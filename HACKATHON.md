# MARSWALK — Hackathon Playbook (NASA Space Apps)

## The 90-second pitch

> NASA has been mapping Mars for fifty years — but the data lives in silos: a DEM here,
> a mineral map there, rover weather in another portal. The first crew on Mars needs **one
> screen** that turns all of it into a decision: *where do we walk today?*
>
> **MARSWALK** is that screen. We load the real 20-metre CTX elevation model of Jezero
> Crater — 90 megabytes of the same mosaic the Mars 2020 team used for landing-site
> analysis — and layer it with THEMIS thermal imaging, MOLA context and a CRISM-informed
> mineral model. Three machine-learning models sit on top: a traversability predictor
> that agrees with EVA engineering constraints **99.1%** of the time, a science-value
> regressor, and a seasonal dust model that calls GO or HOLD for the EVA.
>
> The clock in the header is not decoration: we compute Mars solar longitude from Kepler's
> equation and **validated it against 4,745 sols of real REMS telemetry — every single sol
> number reproduced, and the Ls residual is at the precision floor of the instrument**.
>
> Pick two science targets and our A\* planner routes between them around >20° slopes on
> an 80-metre grid, returning distance, grade profile, oxygen, water and power budgets with
> explicit advisories in under a second. Live NASA feeds bring in today's solar-particle
> activity and fresh surface imagery; a one-click 3D flythrough and a machine-readable EVA
> export close the loop.
>
> Real mission data, real ML, one layered map — **MARSWALK: the Interplanetary Survival
> Guide for the first Marswalk.**

## Live demo script (3 minutes)

1. **Boot** — app opens and auto-plans a route from HAB-1 (the Perseverance landing site)
   to the two highest-value ML targets. Read the numbers off the stats strip: *"That route
   was computed on real Jezero topography — distance, EVA hours and the grade profile
   below, and zero advisories."*
2. **Data provenance (15 s)** — point at the top-right chip and the header: **SOL, Ls,
   LMST**. *"Ls is computed from Kepler's equation and checked against 4,745 sols of
   NASA's REMS telemetry. Every sol number matches; the Ls error is at the instrument's
   own precision floor."*
3. **Layers (20 s)** — switch base CTX Terrain → CTX Ortho → THEMIS Day-IR; toggle
   Mineral Prospectivity: *"clays on the delta, carbonates on the margin — placed from
   peer-reviewed Jezero geology, and labelled 'modelled' in the legend because it is."*
4. **ML (20 s)** — toggle ML Traversability, hover a red flank and read the probe HUD:
   *"SLOPE 28°, TRAVERSE HOLD — the classifier agrees with EVA slope limits 99.1% of the
   time."* Add a target card to the route.
5. **Plan (15 s)** — click +WAYPOINT, hit **PLAN EVA ROUTE**, then raise crew to 3 and
   watch the O₂ and water budgets jump.
6. **Conditions (15 s)** — conditions panel: dust season, radiation advisory from real
   DONKI flares **and solar-energetic-particle events**, EVA GREEN. *"The measured
   temperature and pressure next to it are actual surface weather, not a model."*
7. **Live + 3D + export (25 s)** — LIVE FEED tab: NASA imagery and today's solar activity.
   3D TERRAIN: orbit the crater with the route and targets. EXPORT EVA PLAN: JSON with
   everything, including the ML metrics and the consumables basis.

## Q&A prep (judges will ask)

**"Is the mineral map real CRISM data?"**
*"No, and we say so in the UI. It's a CRISM-informed prospectivity model: unit geometry is
placed from peer-reviewed Jezero geology — delta phyllosilicates, margin olivine-carbonates,
mafic floor — driven through fractal fields and the real DEM. It's labelled 'modelled' in
the legend. Swapping in pixel-wise CRISM rasters from PDS Geosciences is a data-loading
change because layers are just grids behind the tile server."*

**"How do you know your Mars clock is right?"**
*"We tested it against the instruments. Ls comes from Kepler's equation with JPL orbital
elements, and we fitted the constants on the older half of the REMS record, then scored on
the newer half — held-out RMS 0.286°, which is exactly the floor that REMS's whole-degree
reporting imposes. Against MEDA, which reports decimals, the RMS is 0.107°. And our sol
numbering reproduces 4,745 out of 4,745 published sols. `scripts/validate_mars_time.py`
re-runs the whole check live."*

**"Is the weather live?"**
*"Two different things, and we keep them separate. The measured temperature, pressure and
UV come from NASA's REMS instrument — 4,745 sols of real surface weather, binned by Ls, and
they're displayed as measured. The dust risk is a different thing: that's published Mars
dust-storm climatology, because no live opacity measurement is available to us. We label
each accordingly rather than dressing climatology up as a measurement."*

**"Why not NASA WorldWind or Cesium?"**
*"We needed pixel-level fusion of our ML rasters with the DEM under a mission-control
aesthetic, plus offline reliability on stage. Our canvas pyramid renders 11 layers with
per-overlay opacity and continuous zoom without any CDN dependency. The geometry stays
equirectangular, and we document that the projection scales longitude by cos(latitude), so
porting to WorldWind is straightforward."*

**"What if the Wi-Fi dies on stage?"**
*"Every live feed degrades instead of failing: the NASA image API falls back to 12 real
images bundled in the repo, weather and space-weather fall back to on-disk snapshots, and
images are served from our own origin through a cached proxy rather than hot-linked — so a
client network policy that blocks the NASA CDN can't break the gallery."*

**"What do the consumables numbers mean?"**
*"Documented planning estimates, and the export carries the basis: 0.075 kg O₂ per
crew-hour, 0.22 L water, 0.20 kW suit power against a 1.6 kWh PLSS — about 8 hours of
suited operations, which is the endurance NASA's exploration EVA suit work has targeted.
The profile colours and advisories trigger as you approach those limits."*

**"What's the model accuracy?"**
*"Traversability: 99.13% agreement with the clean EVA engineering rule. The lower number,
77.97%, is against deliberately noised labels — that gap is the entropy we injected on
purpose. Science regressor: R² = 0.851 on a held-out 20% of 250,000 terrain samples. Both
are written to `models/verification.json` by a script you can re-run."*

**"What did you get wrong along the way?"** (a good question to have an answer for)
*"Four things we found and fixed: the DEM void-fill wrote 0 m — a spike the height of a
small mountain — into any wide data gap; our A\* heuristic was inadmissible once science
weighting was on, so it returned longer routes than necessary; the slope limit was a soft
penalty so the planner would cross a 30° wall to save distance; and the suit-power figure
made every route raise an advisory, which meant the warning couldn't distinguish a marginal
EVA from a comfortable one. All four are in the correctness notes in the README."*

## What YOUR team should do

1. **Register** on spaceappschallenge.org, create/join the team, add this challenge
   ("Interplanetary Survival Guide: Martian Map").
2. **Use your own NASA API key.** One is already wired in for development, but keys are
   free at api.nasa.gov and you should not ship someone else's. Set `NASA_API_KEY` in the
   environment, or replace the value in `app/config.py`. Do not publish a repo containing
   a live key.
3. When the hackathon opens, check the challenge **Resources tab** for dataset links NASA
   experts added — new rasters can be wired in as a layer within an hour because layers are
   just grids behind the tile server.
4. Build **3–4 slides** from `shots/` plus the pitch above. If you want fresh screenshots,
   run the app and capture them yourself — the numbers on screen will be current.
5. **Re-run the verification** before you present, so every number you quote is one you
   just reproduced:
   ```bash
   python3 scripts/validate_mars_time.py     # Mars clock vs live NASA telemetry
   python3 scripts/build_verification.py     # regenerate models/verification.json
   ```

## Repo quick facts (for the submission form)

- **Stack:** Python / FastAPI · NumPy / SciPy / scikit-learn · custom JS canvas engine · three.js
- **Data:** USGS/JPL CTX 20 m DEM (checksum-pinned) · CTX ortho browse · NASA Mars Trek WMTS
  (THEMIS, MOLA) · NASA MEDA & REMS weather · NASA DONKI (FLR/SEP/CME/GST) · NASA Image Library
- **Models:** HistGB classifier, HistGB regressor, GBM dust climatology, k-means target
  selection with a geology-coverage pass
- **Scale:** 5.6 M-cell working grid (40 m), 1.4 M-cell planning grid (80 m), 11 map layers
- **Tests:** `scripts/smoke_test.py` walks boot, layers, probe, routing, live feed, 3D and
  export in headless Chromium (needs `playwright install chromium`), plus the Mars-clock
  validation against live NASA telemetry and the verification build
